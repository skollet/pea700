Problem 1: Logistic growth (5 points)

A population \(u(t)\) grows with the rate \(r > 0\) as long as it is small, and its growth stops at the carrying capacity \(K > 0\):

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t} = r \, u \left( 1 - \frac{u}{K} \right), \qquad u(0) = u_0 , \qquad 0 < u_0 < K . \]

  1. Solve the equation by separation of variables. Use the partial fraction decomposition \(\dfrac{1}{u (1 - u/K)} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{K - u}\) (check it first). Show that \[ u(t) = \frac{K}{1 + \left( K / u_0 - 1 \right) e^{-r t}} . \]

  2. Take \(r = 1\), \(K = 10\), \(u_0 = 1\), and integrate to \(t = 5\) with the forward and the backward Euler method for the time steps \(\Delta t = 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625, 0.03125\). For backward Euler, write down the equation \(G(U) = 0\) for \(U = u_{n+1}\) and its derivative \(G'(U)\), and solve it with Newton’s method. Compute the signed errors \(u_n - u(5)\) at \(t = 5\) and the observed orders \(p = \log_2 \big( |E(\Delta t)| / |E(\Delta t / 2)| \big)\). Do the orders agree with the theory? Compare the signs of the errors of the two methods and explain them with the local truncation errors of section 5.4.

Problem 2: Cooling with a daily cycle (4 points)

A body with temperature \(T(t)\) (in °C, time \(t\) in hours) exchanges heat with its surroundings, whose temperature \(T_a(t)\) follows a daily cycle:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}t} = -k \, \big( T - T_a(t) \big), \qquad T_a(t) = 15 + 5 \sin \frac{2 \pi t}{24} , \qquad T(0) = 25 , \]

with the exchange coefficient \(k = 0.2 \ \mathrm{h}^{-1}\). Unlike the equations in the script, the tendency depends on \(t\).

  1. Write down one step of the backward Euler method for this equation and solve it for \(T_{n+1}\). Why is no Newton iteration needed here?

  2. Integrate for three days, \(0 \le t \le 72\) h, with forward and backward Euler, for \(\Delta t = 1\) h and \(\Delta t = 12\) h. As a reference, use forward Euler with \(\Delta t = 0.001\) h; since the method is of first order, its error is about 1000 times smaller than with \(\Delta t = 1\) h. Plot the results and explain what each method does at \(\Delta t = 12\) h. Hint: print the ambient temperature \(T_a(t_n)\) at the times \(t_n\) that the methods use.
