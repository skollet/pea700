Exercise 6: Stability and stiffness

Prognostic Modelling (pea700), WiSe 2026/27

Out: week 6 · Back: week 7 · 9 points

Covers unit 6 of the script: the amplification factor (section 6.2), stability regions, A- and L-stability (section 6.3) and stiff problems (section 6.5). Start in the lab and finish at home. Problem 1 is done with pen and paper. Problem 2 is done on the computer, with the matrix of the method of lines and the NumPy functions np.linalg.eigh and np.linalg.solve used in the script.

Problem 1: A family of one-step methods (5 points)

Forward Euler, backward Euler and the trapezoidal method belong to one family. For a weight \(\sigma\) with \(0 \le \sigma \le 1\), the weighted method evaluates the tendency \(f\) at the start and at the end of the step and combines the two values with the weights \(1 - \sigma\) and \(\sigma\):

\[ u_{n+1} = u_n + \Delta t \, \Big[ (1 - \sigma) \, f(t_n, u_n) + \sigma \, f(t_{n+1}, u_{n+1}) \Big] . \]

Here \(u_n\) is the numerical solution at time \(t_n = n \, \Delta t\) and \(\Delta t > 0\) the time step. For \(\sigma = 0\) this is forward Euler, for \(\sigma = 1\) backward Euler, for \(\sigma = 1/2\) the trapezoidal method.

  1. Apply the method to the test equation \(\mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}t = \mu u\) and show that its amplification factor is \[ R(z) = \frac{1 + (1 - \sigma) \, z}{1 - \sigma \, z} , \qquad z = \mu \Delta t . \] Check the three special cases against the script.

  2. Write \(z = a + i b\) with real \(a\), \(b\). Show that \(|R(z)| \le 1\) is equivalent to \[ 2 a + (1 - 2 \sigma) \, (a^2 + b^2) \le 0 . \] Conclude: the method is A-stable if and only if \(\sigma \ge 1/2\). For \(\sigma < 1/2\), determine the largest stable time step for the decay equation \(\mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}t = -k u\) with \(k > 0\).

  3. Compute \(\lim_{|z| \to \infty} R(z)\) for \(\sigma > 0\). Which member of the family is L-stable? For the decay equation, \(z = -k\Delta t\) with \(k > 0\). For which \(k \Delta t\) is \(R \ge 0\), so that the numerical solution decays without changing its sign, as the exact solution does? What does this mean for the trapezoidal method with a large time step?

  4. Expand \(R(z)\) in powers of \(z\) up to \(z^2\) (geometric series, as in section 6.2) and compare with \(e^z\). Show that the method is of order 2 for \(\sigma = 1/2\) and of order 1 otherwise.

Problem 2: Diffusion is stiff (4 points)

Diffusion with \(D = 1\) on \(0 \le x \le 1\), \(u = 0\) at both ends, is discretized in space as in section 5.1: \(N = 19\) interior nodes \(x_j = j h\), \(h = 1/(N+1)\), and \(\mathrm{d}\vec u/\mathrm{d}t = -A \vec u\) with the matrix \(A\) of the method of lines. The initial state is a step, \(u_j(0) = 1\) for \(0.25 < x_j < 0.75\) and \(u_j(0) = 0\) otherwise.

  1. Compute the smallest and the largest eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_N\) of \(A\) from the formula of section 6.1, the stiffness ratio \(S = \lambda_N / \lambda_1\), and the largest stable time step of forward Euler. How many forward Euler steps are needed at least to reach \(t = 0.16\)?

  2. Integrate with forward Euler to \(t = 0.156\) with \(\Delta t = 0.0012\) and \(\Delta t = 0.0013\). As reference, compute the exact solution of the system of ODEs with the eigenvectors of \(A\) (np.linalg.eigh, as in the script): \(\vec u(t) = \sum_m e^{-\lambda_m t} \, c_m(0) \, \vec v_m\), where \(\vec v_m\) are the normalized eigenvectors and \(c_m(0) = \vec v_m \cdot \vec u(0)\). Explain the result with the amplification factor of the fastest component.

  3. Write down one step of backward Euler and one step of the trapezoidal method (section 5.5) for \(\mathrm{d}\vec u/\mathrm{d}t = -A \vec u\), each as a linear system for \(\vec u_{n+1}\). The trapezoidal method applied to the diffusion equation is called the Crank–Nicolson method. Integrate with both methods with \(\Delta t = 0.02\), eight steps to \(t = 0.16\), and solve the linear system in each step with np.linalg.solve. Plot both solutions after one step and after eight steps, together with the reference. Explain what you see with the amplification factors at \(z_1 = -\lambda_1 \Delta t\) and \(z_N = -\lambda_N \Delta t\), and with Problem 1 c).
